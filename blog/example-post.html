Writing a coverage-guided fuzzer from scratch

A walk through building a small coverage-guided fuzzer, the bugs it surfaced, and the things I'd do differently next time.

This is a placeholder post. Replace the content with your own writing; the styling below shows how headings, code, quotes, and lists will render.

Why build one

Off-the-shelf fuzzers are excellent, but writing a small one from scratch is one of the fastest ways to understand how coverage feedback actually drives input mutation.

The point isn't to beat AFL. The point is to internalize the feedback loop that makes it work.

The core loop

A minimal coverage-guided loop looks roughly like this:

while True:
    seed = corpus.pick()
    candidate = mutate(seed)
    cov = run(target, candidate)
    if cov.has_new_edges():
        corpus.add(candidate)

What it found

  • An off-by-one in the length-prefix parser
  • A panic on malformed UTF-8 in the header
  • Two unbounded allocations from attacker-controlled sizes

What I'd change

Next time I'd invest earlier in corpus minimization and a smarter mutation scheduler; most of the wasted cycles came from redundant inputs.
